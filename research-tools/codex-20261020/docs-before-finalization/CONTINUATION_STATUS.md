5. **Causal automatic tone count/confidence (Claude, 16 Oct 2026).** A multitaper line test with acquire/release hysteresis decides how many tones exist (0 or more), and a separate mute gate checks only already-matured forecasts. Settings were frozen on development scenes; 12 fresh scene types were tested. It stays silent on noise where the old top-3 detector invented 3 tones, follows drift (+18/+13 dB versus about 0), and picks up new tones within 1.25 s. Weaknesses: about 1 s of slightly negative gain after tones stop; weak tones in strong noise are missed. On the failed clean fan trial it finds no lines and outputs zero (0.00 dB): harm avoided, not a success. See [TONE_SELECTION.md](TONE_SELECTION.md).
6. **Faster tone release with repeats (Claude, 16 Oct 2026).** Tuned on 36 development scenes only, then tested on 5 fresh random frequency draws × 12 scenes. The tuned settings (v2: 0.3 s tracker window, short-window amplitude release, shared 5-block gate) raise the tones-stop score from 4.8 to 8.2 dB (20 ms horizon), tying prefix AR, and drift from 18.4 to 27.4 dB. Costs: 0.1–0.5 dB on steady tones, and 1.2 dB on tones in rumble from the gate. Right after a stop, the gain is still about −5 dB for 1.5 s. See [TONE_RELEASE.md](TONE_RELEASE.md).
7. **Real hums from other people (Claude, 16 Oct 2026).** Nineteen Wikimedia Commons recordings (fans, ventilation, air conditioners, compressors and others), downloaded with the user's approval and kept local. Scored at the recording microphone with v1/v2 frozen. Whole 35–350 Hz band: v2 + gate median 0.3 dB, never below −0.5 dB; at the tones themselves 2.9 dB, against 6.3 dB for an AR model adapted on each file's prefix. Most real low-frequency hum is broadband: a perfect tones-only canceller would remove a median 1.9 dB. See [REAL_HUMS.md](REAL_HUMS.md).
8. **Tune by ear, simulated (Claude, 17 Oct 2026).** The listener's ear finds each tone's anti-hum gain and phase with A/B questions; a final on/off check switches off anything not heard to help. In 48 simulated rooms with the real recordings as sources (protocol frozen first): median 0.3 dB at the tones, 13.2 dB on the one strongly tonal ventilation recording, and boosts rarer than without the check (7% against 18%). The limits are the real-tone forecast and audibility (median cue 1.4 dB), not the question procedure. This is simulated ear attenuation only. See [TUNE_BY_EAR.md](TUNE_BY_EAR.md).

10. **Real-tone forecaster study (Claude, 18 Oct 2026).** Recordings split by author into development (8 files) and test (10 files), with the choice rule written before any development run. It was better on all 8 test files with tones and ahead on the whole band on 9 of 10 (median 0.49 against 0.27 dB). It failed the frozen bar: 6 third-octave boosts against v2's 4, all at most +1.7 dB. v2 stays in the engine. Not independent: every file was analysed in `results-real-hums-01` first.
