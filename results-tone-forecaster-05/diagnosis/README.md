# Study 05 diagnosis: can the joint check react faster to sudden changes and to the start?

Development material only: the eight study-01 development recordings (`results-tone-forecaster-01/split.json`), the 44 development synthetic scenes and study 03's 8 buzz scenes. No study-01 test recording, study-02 fresh recording (including Buzzing_Speakers.ogg), synthetic test scene or new recording was read for anything in this folder. `scripts/s5_draw_check.py` read study 02's synthetic test *frequency lists* (numbers only, no scene was made or scored) to see whether they could be avoided. These are design trials, run before the development rule was written; the formal comparison is `../development/dev-01`.

Reference-signal prediction only. Nothing here was played or measured at an ear.

## The question

Study 03 (`../../results-tone-forecaster-03/`) left the joint check (study 02, `anc_lab/tone_floors.py`) with 25 development boosts (a third-octave band 1 dB or more louder). Fourteen were late windows on the sudden-change scenes (a tone appears at 9 s, the tones stop at 9 s, or one source gives way to another at 9 s), and one was on the 5 s mains clip before the check's first decision. Its guess was that the check's 8 s of medians react too slowly. This study asked whether the check can react faster to a change and to the start of a recording without losing its tone gain over v2.

## What this means

The boosts on the change scenes are not a reaction-time problem. Even a check that knew the instant of the change would leave most of them, because the output switching a strong tone on or off in one block puts a second edge into the error, and that edge spreads into the next band. A fade at each switch removes most of them, in the ideal forecaster and in the real check alike; a faster reaction on top adds little.

The start case is a property of the start setting, not of speed, and the one setting that removes it there costs more elsewhere.

The fades also change what the band check sees on recordings where the trackers churn, and on development material that raised the whole-band gain. This was not designed for and is reported as a development finding only; the test checks it as a secondary result.

## Candidates taken forward

Raised-cosine fades of 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 s, a 0.1 s linear fade, a 0.1 s fade with a 0.3 s cross-fade between settings, and a 0.1 s fade with a 0.2 s start floor, to the formal development run, under the rule in `anc_lab/forecaster_study_05.DEVELOPMENT_RULE` (also in `../development/dev-01/DEVELOPMENT.md`), written after these trials and before that run. On these trials the 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 s fades, the linear fade and the switch fade would meet its checks (a) to (g), and the start-floor variant would fail the synthetic check. The trial kept only each method's worst-run timing, so the timing check (h) is first applied in the formal run.

The scripts were run from a scratch folder with the project path as the first argument and are kept as they ran. Trial timings depend on what else the machine is doing (another long job shared it), so the rule compares each candidate with the joint check in the same run.
